# ISO vs. COSO vs. CMMI: How to Use These Frameworks in Your New GRC Job

Starting a new job in Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC), IT Risk, or Information Security can be challenging. You may find existing policies, incomplete risk registers, recurring audit findings, unclear control ownership, and processes that work differently across departments.

The question is: **Where should you start, and which framework should you use?**

Three useful frameworks and standards to understand are ISO 27001, COSO, and CMMI. Although they serve different purposes, using them together can help you build a more structured, measurable, and effective GRC program.

## 1. ISO 27001: Build and Maintain Information Security Governance

ISO/IEC 27001 provides requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Information Security Management System (ISMS).

When joining a new organization, ISO 27001 is particularly useful if your responsibilities include information security governance, compliance, risk assessments, or certification readiness.

**How to use it in your new job:**

- Review existing information security policies and procedures.
- Understand the organization's information security risks and risk treatment plans.
- Review the Statement of Applicability (SoA) and applicable Annex A controls.
- Verify control implementation and collect supporting evidence.
- Track audit findings, corrective actions, and improvement activities.

**Practical example:**

Suppose you discover that employee access reviews are not consistently completed. You can assess the relevant information security risks, verify the applicable access-control requirements, document the review process, assign control owners, and track evidence of completion.

**Key deliverables:** Risk register, risk treatment plan, Statement of Applicability, control evidence tracker, and corrective action plan.

## 2. COSO: Strengthen Internal Controls and Risk Governance

COSO's Internal Control–Integrated Framework helps organizations evaluate and improve their internal control systems.

While ISO 27001 focuses on an information security management system, COSO provides a broader perspective on internal control, governance, accountability, and monitoring.

Its five components are:

1. Control Environment
2. Risk Assessment
3. Control Activities
4. Information and Communication
5. Monitoring Activities

**How to use it in your new job:**

- Evaluate whether important controls are appropriately designed.
- Identify gaps in control ownership and accountability.
- Review how management monitors control effectiveness.
- Assess whether risks are identified and communicated appropriately.
- Help management prioritize and remediate control deficiencies.

**Practical example:**

Suppose a financial process allows vendor bank-account details to be changed without an independent review. You can assess the risk, evaluate the existing control, establish appropriate approval and verification procedures, and define monitoring responsibilities.

**Key deliverables:** Risk and control matrix (RCM), control assessment, control owner matrix, deficiency tracker, and management reporting.

## 3. CMMI: Improve Process Maturity and Consistency

CMMI is a capability and process-improvement model that helps organizations evaluate and improve how their processes are managed.

In a GRC environment, it can help identify why the same audit findings continue to appear, why evidence collection is inconsistent, or why remediation activities depend too heavily on individual employees.

**How to use it in your new job:**

- Review whether critical processes are documented and repeatable.
- Identify inconsistent execution across departments.
- Establish standard workflows, responsibilities, and escalation procedures.
- Define measurable performance indicators.
- Use performance results and lessons learned to drive improvement.

**Practical example:**

Suppose every department performs access reviews differently. Some teams maintain spreadsheets, others rely on email, and some cannot provide complete evidence during audits.

You can standardize the review procedure, establish clear ownership, define completion deadlines, create a common evidence repository, and monitor completion rates.

**Key deliverables:** Standard operating procedures, process maps, RACI matrix, KPI dashboard, process improvement plan, and recurring-issue analysis.

Note that CMMI's maturity model and appraisal approach should be applied according to the relevant CMMI model and organizational context; a GRC team should not claim formal CMMI maturity levels without an appropriate basis.

## 4. How to Use All Three Together

Consider a common GRC problem: privileged access reviews are repeatedly overdue, and auditors keep raising the same finding.

Here is how each framework can contribute:

**Step 1 — ISO 27001: Identify the security requirements**

Understand the risks associated with privileged access, identify applicable controls, document the required review process, and establish evidence requirements.

**Step 2 — COSO: Evaluate the control**

Determine whether the control is appropriately designed, responsibilities are clear, reviews are performed and evidenced, and monitoring detects failures.

**Step 3 — CMMI: Improve the process**

Standardize the workflow, define review deadlines and escalation rules, establish performance metrics, and use trend analysis to reduce recurring failures.

The outcome is not simply another policy. It is a more consistent process supported by clear accountability, reliable evidence, and measurable results.

## 5. Your First 30 Days in a New GRC Role

If you are joining a new organization, avoid trying to redesign everything immediately. Start by understanding the environment and identifying the highest-priority risks.

**Week 1: Understand the current state**

Review policies, audit reports, risk registers, regulatory requirements, control inventories, and open remediation items.

**Week 2: Assess control effectiveness**

Identify high-risk gaps, recurring audit findings, missing evidence, and unclear control ownership.

**Week 3: Improve process consistency**

Prioritize a critical process, document the workflow, establish responsibilities, and define appropriate evidence and performance measures.

**Week 4: Present a practical improvement plan**

Share the key risks, remediation priorities, accountable owners, target dates, and metrics with management.

Your goal is to demonstrate that GRC is not just about collecting evidence before an audit. It is about helping the organization understand risks, operate effective controls, and continuously improve.

## 6. What Should GRC Professionals Remember?

ISO 27001, COSO, and CMMI are not competing frameworks. They address different needs:

- **ISO 27001:** How do we establish and maintain an effective information security management system?
- **COSO:** How do we evaluate and strengthen internal controls and risk governance?
- **CMMI:** How do we improve the consistency, capability, and performance of our processes?

The best choice depends on your organization's objectives, regulatory obligations, existing control environment, and business priorities. You do not need to implement every framework in every organization.

## Final Thoughts

A successful GRC professional does more than understand standards and frameworks. They know how to translate requirements into practical controls, identify weaknesses, establish accountability, and demonstrate measurable improvement.

When joining a new organization, start with the business risks, understand the existing controls, and choose the framework that addresses the actual problem.

**Remember: ISO helps structure the security management system, COSO strengthens the internal-control perspective, and CMMI helps improve process capability. Used appropriately, they can complement one another and make GRC more effective.**
