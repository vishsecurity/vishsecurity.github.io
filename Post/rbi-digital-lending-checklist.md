# RBI Digital Lending Compliance: A Practical Audit Checklist for NBFCs

**Published:** October 2026 | **Category:** RBI / Digital Lending / GRC

---

## Why this matters

Digital lending continues to expand across India, but the regulatory bar is also rising. For NBFCs and fintech partners, the focus is no longer only on product speed or customer acquisition; it is equally about governance, data handling, vendor controls, and audit readiness.

The RBI's expectations around digital lending sit at the intersection of technology, risk management, consumer protection, and operational discipline. In practice, that means a strong control environment has to be built before a lender can confidently scale.

---

## Core control areas to review

### 1. Customer onboarding and KYC

- Verify that digital onboarding aligns with RBI KYC and AML requirements.
- Confirm that PAN, Aadhaar, and other identity data are validated through approved workflows.
- Ensure exceptions and manual review cases are logged, assigned, and monitored.
- Review whether onboarding controls are consistent across web, mobile, and partner channels.

### 2. Consent and data minimization

- Check whether consent is explicit, informed, and time-stamped.
- Validate that personal data is collected only for defined business purposes.
- Verify that consent records are retained and can be produced during audit and regulatory review.
- Review the data retention and deletion process for inactive applications and rejected borrowers.

### 3. Credit decisioning and underwriting

- Confirm the logic behind underwriting decisions is documented and explainable.
- Ensure model risk and change control are tracked, especially for automated decision engines.
- Review the escalation process for manual underwriting exceptions and adverse outcomes.
- Validate whether no-discrimination, fair-lending, and customer communication principles are embedded.

### 4. Disbursement, servicing, and collections

- Review payment initiation controls and channel-level segregation of duties.
- Verify that disbursement, repayment, and collection workflows are monitored for anomalies.
- Confirm that collections activities comply with fair treatment and communication standards.
- Ensure periodic review of outsourced collection partners and channel vendors.

### 5. Third-party and vendor governance

- Map all fintech, cloud, KYC, and payment support vendors to risk classifications.
- Review contractual controls, SLA obligations, security requirements, and audit rights.
- Ensure vendor assessments cover operational resilience, data privacy, and incident reporting obligations.
- Verify remediation tracking for all identified vendor control gaps.

---

## Governance checklist for internal audit teams

A practical RBI assessment checklist should include:

- governance structure and decision rights
- policy coverage and exceptions register
- risk assessment and risk treatment summaries
- board and committee oversight records
- BCP/DR testing evidence
- IT outsourcing records and due diligence
- incident response and escalation logs
- vulnerability and patch management evidence
- access review records and privileged-account monitoring
- data localization and cross-border transfer reviews

---

## What regulators typically look for

The strongest digital lending programs usually demonstrate:

- clear accountability at the product and technology levels
- documented policies and operational procedures
- evidence of periodic control testing
- measurable remediation closure for audit findings
- robust vendor and outsourcing oversight
- customer-centric controls with documented risk ownership

This is especially important where the organization is operating both as a lender and as a technology-enabled service provider.

---

## Practical recommendation

The most effective approach is not to treat compliance as a separate project. Instead, map RBI expectations to your existing risk and control framework, then convert them into operating procedures, evidence templates, and management reporting dashboards.

In other words: digitize the control environment, not just the lending product.

---

## Closing thought

Digital lending can scale rapidly, but scalable compliance requires governance that is disciplined, evidence-backed, and connected to real operating risk. If the control design is strong, regulatory readiness becomes more manageable, and the business can grow with greater confidence.
