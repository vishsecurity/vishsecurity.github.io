# From GRC Professional to High-Paid Cybersecurity Risk Expert: Skills That Make the Difference

In cybersecurity, learning compliance frameworks is important. But if you want to move into higher-paying GRC and IT Risk roles, knowing the standards alone is not enough.

The real value comes from your ability to identify business risks, evaluate controls, challenge gaps, influence stakeholders, and help management make informed decisions.

Here are seven skills worth developing.

**1. Advanced IT Risk Management**

Learn how to build risk registers, assess likelihood and impact, evaluate inherent and residual risk, define treatment plans, and document risk acceptance. More importantly, explain how a technical weakness can affect business operations, customers, and regulatory obligations.

**2. Control Testing and Audit Quality**

Move beyond checking whether a policy exists. Learn control design assessment, operating effectiveness testing, sampling, evidence validation, root cause analysis, and remediation verification.

**3. Regulatory and Framework Mapping**

Understand how to map ISO/IEC 27001 controls to applicable regulatory requirements, including relevant RBI directions and PCI DSS where applicable. Learn to identify overlaps, gaps, evidence requirements, and accountable control owners without treating different frameworks as interchangeable.

**4. Technical Cybersecurity Knowledge**

Develop practical knowledge of IAM, privileged access, vulnerability management, cloud security, logging and monitoring, incident response, backup and recovery, and change management. A strong GRC professional should understand how controls actually work—not just how they are documented.

**5. GRC Automation and Data Analysis**

Build proficiency in Excel, Power Query, Power BI, and GRC platforms. Learn to automate evidence tracking, monitor overdue findings, identify recurring control failures, and present meaningful risk trends.

**6. Risk Reporting and Stakeholder Management**

Senior professionals are expected to communicate risk clearly, challenge inadequate remediation, negotiate realistic action plans, and present concise management reports. The ability to turn technical findings into business decisions is a valuable differentiator.

**7. AI Governance and Emerging Risk**

Learn AI risk management, data governance, accountability, third-party AI risk, and ISO/IEC 42001. Build an understanding of how organizations can govern AI systems responsibly and assess emerging risks.

## Build Proof, Not Just Knowledge

Instead of collecting certifications without applying them, build practical artifacts that demonstrate your capabilities:

- An IT risk register with treatment plans and residual risk ratings.
- A control testing workbook with evidence and test conclusions.
- An audit finding tracker with root cause, corrective action, and closure validation.
- A compliance mapping matrix.
- A management dashboard showing overdue high-risk findings and control effectiveness trends.

Use fictional or sanitized data when building portfolio projects.

## A Practical 90-Day Strategy

**Days 1–30:** Strengthen risk assessment, control testing, and regulatory mapping.

**Days 31–60:** Build practical GRC artifacts and automate reporting using Excel or Power BI.

**Days 61–90:** Practice risk-based interview scenarios, prepare examples of measurable impact, and target roles aligned with your strongest skills.

## The Real Career Advantage

Higher-paying opportunities are generally associated with greater responsibility, specialized expertise, sound judgment, and the ability to influence business decisions. No single certification or tool guarantees a salary increase.

The goal should be to progress from documenting compliance to owning risk outcomes, improving control effectiveness, and advising management.

**My takeaway:** Don't just learn GRC. Learn how to implement it, measure it, improve it, and communicate its business value.
